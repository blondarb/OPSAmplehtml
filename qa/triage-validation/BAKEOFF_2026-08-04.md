## Limitations — read before quoting any number

1. **This measures engine vs. these labels, not engine vs. truth.** The labels were set by
   Steve (DO) on 2026-07-20 (`f54e391`) — a real clinical pass, but the commit records that it
   was done "against his own clinical judgment **and an offline run of each note through the
   real triage engine**." The rater saw the engine's answer while labelling. That is
   **unblinded**, and it is a single rater with no inter-rater reliability. Until the labels
   are re-graded blind by two independent raters, "11 disagree" bounds the *disagreement*, not
   the *error*.
2. **The 5 `emergent` cases are unmeasured.** `emergent_override` is not stored in
   `validation_cases`, so the offline replay cannot reconstruct them and reports N/A. That is
   the highest-acuity fifth of the corpus — including the stroke alerts — and it is currently
   invisible to this method. Fixing it is a measurement change, not an engine change.
3. **n = 21 is small.** One case moves the agreement rate by ~5%. Treat differences under
   ~3 cases as noise.
4. **The engine is nondeterministic on borderline notes.** The same note produced 3.00
   (`semi_urgent`) and 3.50 (`urgent`) three minutes apart in production on 2026-08-04. This
   table uses the temperature-0 baseline run per case where available; a re-run will not
   reproduce it exactly. That is a property of the engine, documented on `/triage`, not a
   defect in this measurement.
5. **The `red_flag_override` floor cannot fire in offline replay** (that field is not stored),
   so floor-driven counts are a **lower bound**. The four dimension-score floors are faithful.
6. **This table never sees the emergency gateway.** `run-validation.ts` calls Bedrock directly
   with the scoring prompt and never calls `runEmergencyGateway` — the deterministic lexical
   system the live `/triage` page runs *before* scoring. A case can agree with its label here
   and still show the full-screen emergency takeover on stage. See the note below.

## Known emergency-gateway false positives — do-not-demo (added 2026-08-04)

**One label changed, and not by this review.** `cross-10-okafor` and `cross-11-petrov` keep
`expectedTier: "urgent"` on the AI review below. `packet-nakamura` was relabelled
`urgent` → `emergent` on **2026-08-05 by Steve Arbogast, DO**, reading the note himself: a
relative afferent pupillary defect present *now*, together with bilateral leg weakness, is a
focal neurological deficit that belongs in an ED. His words: *"with the bilateral leg
weakness, that suggests focal neurologic deficits, probably emergent and probably should go
to the ER."*

**That inverts what this review concluded about that case.** Two AI passes had called
Nakamura's emergency firing a false positive on historical language. It was not: the gateway
was **right**, and the *label* was wrong. Recorded prominently because it is a caution about
the rest of this section — one of the three cases the review was confident about did not
survive a clinician's read. `cross-10-okafor` and `cross-11-petrov` rest on stronger evidence
(an explicit ROS negation and a conditional ED-return instruction, both unambiguous
misreads), but they carry the same caveat until a human grades them.

This is a single unblinded rater, so it does not satisfy `LABEL_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md` — but it
is a neurologist's clinical judgment rather than an AI pass, which is a materially different
kind of evidence from everything else in this section.

**The problem.** All three cards display `expectedTier=urgent`, but `runEmergencyGateway`
returns `carePathway=emergency_now` on all three — verified live against the current note
text. On stage, clicking any of these three demo cards would show an "urgent" badge and then
immediately contradict it with the full-screen emergency takeover, before any score or rubric
renders.

**The question this raises:** is the label wrong (the note really is emergent), or is the
gateway wrong (it's over-firing on historical/negated/hypothetical language)? Two independent
clinical-review passes — one reasoning as a triage clinician judging the source note directly,
one reasoning specifically about what the gateway's own evidence spans support — were run
against all three cases. **Both passes agreed, on all three cases, that the gateway is
over-firing and the existing `urgent` label is correct.** Relabelling any of them to `emergent`
to match the gateway's stage behavior would have enshrined a detection bug as clinical ground
truth — exactly the trap this document's Limitation #1 already warns about for the label-setting
process itself.

The specific defects, logged here for engineering follow-up (none of this changed
`emergencyGateway.ts`, the rubric, weights, thresholds, or red-flag-override logic — labels and
docs only):

- **`cross-10-okafor`** — 3 of 4 active signals are misreads:
  - `intracranial_hemorrhage_or_sah` fired on the ROS line *"Positive: Progressive headache,
    episodic confusion, transient aphasia, visual phenomena, weight loss, personality change."*
    — contains no hemorrhage/SAH language at all.
  - `acute_cns_infection` fired on the hedged rule-out bullet *"Infectious (TB, fungal, viral
    encephalitis) — less likely but must screen."* — differential/rule-out framing, not a
    present-infection claim.
  - `acute_cerebrovascular` fired on the same ROS recap line, with no acute-stroke-specific
    language, and is contradicted by the treating physician's own non-emergent management (no
    ED referral, no stroke code, LP scheduled 2 days out).
  - The `altered_mental_status_or_coma` signal (one signal carrying 6 evidence spans) is
    grounded in real text (episodic confusion over a 6-week course) but describes a subacute
    history, not a patient who is altered *right now* — today's exam is "Alert, oriented x3 ...
    Motor 5/5 ... Gait normal."
- **`cross-11-petrov`** — 3 of 4 firing signals are misreads:
  - `acute_cerebrovascular` fired on the explicit ROS negation *"No diplopia, no pulsatile
    tinnitus noted today"*, tagged `assertion:"present"` — a negation-detection miss.
  - `intracranial_hemorrhage_or_sah` and `acute_vision_threat` both fired on the same
    conditional ED-return-precaution sentence — *"Instructed patient to go to the Emergency
    Department now if: sudden severe headache, vision loss, diplopia, weakness, or any change
    in mental status"* — a hypothetical/conditional instruction, not a present finding,
    duplicated across two syndrome codes from one sentence.
  - The one grounded signal, `raised_intracranial_pressure` (papilledema, exam-confirmed), is
    real, but the examining ophthalmologist — with the fundus exam and visual fields in hand —
    disposed this as an urgent 48–72h neurology referral, not an ED send.
- **`packet-nakamura`** — **RESOLVED 2026-08-05: NOT a false positive. The gateway was right
  and the label was wrong.** Steve Arbogast, DO read the note and relabelled it
  `urgent` → `emergent`. The caveat this review raised below turned out to be the correct
  reading; the two AI passes that dismissed it were wrong. Everything after this bullet is
  retained as the record of a superseded conclusion — do not cite it as evidence of a gateway
  defect.
  - `acute_vision_threat` (4 evidence spans, one of which comes from a *second* document — the
    MRI report's CLINICAL INDICATION line, *"31-year-old female with right eye vision loss
    (partial recovery), bilateral leg weakness, upper motor neuron signs"*) fired on *"prior
    episode clinically consistent with optic neuritis (right eye pain, vision loss over days,
    partial recovery, now RAPD and reduced color vision)"*, tagged `temporality:"current"`,
    `assertion:"present"`.
  - The suppression machinery is **not** missing for this syndrome: on this very case a second
    `acute_vision_threat` hit *was* suppressed (`temporality:"unknown"`, `suppressed:true`). The
    failure is the temporality classifier scoring this particular span as `current`.
  - **Caveat — why this may not be a false positive at all.** The matched sentence says
    *"**now** RAPD and reduced color vision."* A relative afferent pupillary defect present *now*
    is a current objective finding, not history. Combined with the bilateral leg weakness and
    upper-motor-neuron signs in the same packet, a reader could reasonably call this an acute
    demyelinating presentation (NMOSD/transverse myelitis) that genuinely warrants emergent
    workup. The two review passes judged the label correct; that judgment is **less secure here
    than for okafor and petrov**, and this case in particular should go to the human blind
    re-grade before anyone cites it as a gateway defect.
  - The remaining active signal, `acute_spinal_cord_or_cauda_equina`, is tagged
    `assertion:"uncertain"` by the engine's own logic (verified against the active `signals`
    array — note the *suppressed* `lexicalHits` entry for the same syndrome reads
    `assertion:"present"`, which is easy to misread as a contradiction) and does not
    independently justify `emergency_now`.

## What to do with this

**Blocking, before any calibration change:** re-grade the 21 scorable labels **blind** and
**independently** (see the review protocol handed to Steve). Inter-rater agreement between two
humans is the ceiling on what the engine can be measured against — if two clinicians disagree
on 6 of 21, then an engine disagreeing on 11 of 21 means something very different.

**Then, two separable experiments — not one:**
1. Is `red_flag_presence >= 4 → urgent` too blunt? It drives 4 of 5 floor-driven over-calls.
2. Why did three cases land two tiers below their label? Under-triage is the failure mode with
   no acceptable rate.

**Not before the partner demos (Mayo Aug 7, Novant Aug 12).** Over-triage is the designed
fail-safe direction; the under-call finding makes a rushed calibration change *more*
dangerous, not less. Presentation fixes shipped 2026-08-04 (`e1c01b8`) so a floor-driven tier
is now disclosed on screen rather than contradicting the published arithmetic.
